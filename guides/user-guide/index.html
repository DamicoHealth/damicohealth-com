Creating a new encounter

From the Records screen, tap + New Encounter. The encounter form is a single page covering everything from demographics to notes. Scroll down to fill in each section, or jump between them using the section bar. If your team has more than one encounter form set up (e.g. General vs. Surgery), you'll first pick which one to use.

What's required

Most fields are optional — fill in what's clinically relevant. Saving requires: Given Name, Family Name, Site, Date, Sex, Date of Birth (unless you tick Unknown), and the MRN (which fills itself in). If any are missing, the app lists them when you try to save.

Patient demographics & auto-MRN generation

In the Encounter and Patient cards at the top, enter:

  • Site (a picker), Date, and the auto-filled MRN
  • Given Name and Family Name
  • Sex (Male / Female)
  • Date of Birth — type it as DD/MM/YYYY, or tick Unknown and enter an estimated age instead
  • Phone (optional)

The MRN auto-generates from name and DOB as you fill them out. If the patient has been seen before in your organization, the app detects it and shows a Return Patient banner so you can carry their history forward.

Entering vitals

The Vitals card keeps to the essentials:

  • Temp (°C) — a single number in Celsius (a fever reading is highlighted)
  • BP (mmHg) — one free-text field, e.g. 120/80
  • Weight (kg)
  • Pregnant — N/A / Yes / No
  • Breastfeeding — N/A / Yes / No
Need more vitals?

Heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO2, height, and anything else aren't built into the standard Vitals card. Your admin can add them as custom fields with the form builder (Options → Encounter Forms), and they'll appear on the encounter form for everyone.

Recording labs

Labs use one-tap buttons to keep entry fast.

Binary tests (POS / NEG)

For tests like malaria RDT, pregnancy, HIV rapid, and so on, tap POS or NEG. Tap again to clear.

Numeric tests

For tests with a numeric result (hemoglobin, glucose, etc.), enter the value in the test's field. There's also a free-text Lab Comments box for anything that doesn't fit.

Urinalysis

Open the Urinalysis panel inside the Labs card for a full dipstick: leukocytes, nitrite, urobilinogen, protein, pH, blood, specific gravity, ketones, bilirubin, and glucose. Tap the value for each pad.

Tip

Your admin can add custom lab tests (POS/NEG or numeric) in Options → Lab Tests. Anything you commonly run can be turned into a one-tap button.

Selecting diagnoses

The Diagnosis card gives you a few ways to record a diagnosis:

  • Tap one or more diagnosis preset buttons (your admin curates these). Use the Most Used / A-Z / By System tabs to find them fast.
  • Type into the Working Diagnosis field for anything not in your presets, or to add detail.
  • Optionally add structured ICD-10 codes using the code picker.

There's no "Confirmed / Suspected / Rule out" status flag — capture that wording in the Working Diagnosis text if you need it.

Adding medications

There are two ways to add medications, optimized for different situations.

Rx presets (fastest)

Rx presets are pre-built prescription bundles for common scenarios — for example, "Malaria — adult" adds the drug(s) with the right dose, frequency, and duration in one tap. Tap a preset in the Rx Presets card to drop the whole bundle into the medication builder, then adjust as needed.

Medication builder

For one-off prescriptions or anything not in a preset, tap + Add Medication in the Medications card. Each line has:

  1. Drug — picked from the formulary (grouped by category; the default dose pre-fills)
  2. Dose
  3. Frequency — once, BID, TID, QID, etc.
  4. Duration

The app computes an approximate quantity to dispense from those values. There's no route step and no stock checking — the formulary just supplies the drug list and default doses. Use Additional Treatment Notes below the builder for injections, wound care, or anything patient-specific.

Procedures, imaging, surgical encounters

Further down the form are these optional blocks:

  • Procedures — tap the procedures performed from your preset list (incision & drainage, suturing, dressing, etc.)
  • Imaging — a collapsible card. Choose Ultrasound, pick the ultrasound type (Abdominal, Pelvic, OB/Pregnancy, FAST, etc.), and type the findings. (Ultrasound is the only imaging modality; there's no X-ray option.)
  • Surgical Encounter — a collapsible card. Switch it to Surgery Performed, then enter the Type of Surgery and Surgical Notes. (There's no separate surgeon, anesthesia, or status field.)

Setting referrals with dates

In the Referral card, tap the Referral Type that applies (surgery, follow-up clinic, hospital transfer, etc. — your admin sets the list). Picking a type other than None reveals a Scheduled Date field; set it if you have a date. There's no separate destination or specialty field — add anything extra in the encounter's Notes.

Referrals with a type other than None show up in Scheduling so the team can plan around them.

Save & Next workflow for batch entry

If you're entering a stack of paper forms after a clinic day, use Save & Next (also shown as + Save & Next Patient) instead of Save & Close. This:

  1. Saves the current encounter
  2. Opens a fresh form of the same type
  3. Carries the site and date forward from your last entry
  4. Scrolls back to the top so you can start typing the next patient
Tip

Most teams enter a 4-day clinic of 300+ patients in a few hours using Save & Next. Get into a rhythm and keep your hands on the keyboard.

Searching records

From Records, the search box at the top accepts:

  • Patient name (partial matches work)
  • MRN
  • Diagnosis
  • Medication
  • Site

A Site dropdown sits next to the search box. Tap Filters to reveal more: a From/To date range, Provider, and Referral Type, plus a Today's Patients shortcut. Filters stack — so "everyone seen by Dr. Okafor at Site 3 this week" is one search.

Viewing patient history (multi-visit support)

Tap any patient in the records list to open their chart. All of that patient's visits (matched by MRN) appear newest-first, each as a full block showing that visit's vitals, history, access-to-care, labs, diagnosis, medications, referral, and physician. When there are two or more visits, a small vitals trend table (temp, BP, weight across visits) appears at the top.

Tap Edit on any visit to reopen that encounter. From the patient header, tap + New Visit to start a fresh encounter linked to the same MRN — useful for return patients.

Tracking referral status

Referrals are tracked from the Scheduling screen, not the patient chart. Pick a day on the weekly calendar to list its referrals grouped by type. Each row has a status dropdown — set it to Pending, Completed, No Show, or Cancelled as things happen. The day's tally of completed vs. pending updates as you go.

Tip

Work down a surgery day's list on the Scheduling screen, flipping each referral to Completed or No Show as patients are seen. Tap a patient's name in that list to jump straight to their record.
